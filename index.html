01 — Course Units

Start with the highest-weight units, but build coverage across all five.

UNIT 1 · 15–22%

Foundations of American Democracy

Constitutionalism, democracy, federalism, ratification and founding principles.

UNIT 2 · 25–36%

Interactions Among Branches

Congress, presidency, bureaucracy, judiciary and checks and balances.

UNIT 3 · 13–18%

Civil Liberties & Civil Rights

Rights, liberties, due process, equal protection and civil rights.

UNIT 4 · 10–15%

Political Ideologies & Beliefs

Public opinion, political socialization, ideology and policy preferences.

UNIT 5 · 20–27%

Political Participation

Voting, elections, parties, interest groups, media and participation.

02 — AP Skills

A 4/5 requires more than memorizing vocabulary. Practice these five skills repeatedly.

01 · Concept Application
Apply political concepts to scenarios in context.
02 · SCOTUS Application
Apply Supreme Court decisions to new situations.
03 · Data Analysis
Interpret tables, charts, graphs, maps and infographics.
04 · Source Analysis
Analyze foundational documents and other sources.
05 · Argumentation
Build a defensible argument with evidence and reasoning.

03 — Foundational Documents

For each document: know the argument, evidence, perspective and connection to AP concepts.

Constitution

Structure of government, separation of powers, federalism, checks and balances.

Federalist No. 10

Factions, pluralism and the extended republic.

Federalist No. 51

Ambition counteracting ambition; checks and balances.

Brutus No. 1

Concerns about centralized power and the size of the republic.

Declaration of Independence

Natural rights, equality and consent of the governed.

Letter from Birmingham Jail

Civil disobedience, justice and the relationship between law and morality.

04 — SCOTUS Case Method

Don't memorize cases as isolated names. Use this five-step structure:

1. Facts

What happened?

2. Issue

What constitutional question?

3. Holding

What did the Court decide?

4. Reasoning

Why did it decide that way?

5. Connection

What principle can you apply elsewhere?

06 — FRQ Lab

Practice all four formats used on the AP exam.

Concept Application

Respond to a political scenario and explain a government institution, process or behavior.

Quantitative Analysis

Describe patterns or trends in data and connect them to political concepts.

SCOTUS Comparison

Compare a nonrequired case with a required case.

Argument Essay

Make a defensible claim and support it with evidence and reasoning.

Argument Essay Practice

Prompt: Develop an argument about whether separation of powers makes it easier or harder for the federal government to respond to national problems.

SEPTEMBER

Foundation Month

  • Finish Unit 1 topics
  • Learn Constitution + Federalist No. 10/51 + Brutus No. 1
  • Start SCOTUS case notes
  • 10–15 MCQs per week
Checkpoint: Explain federalism, separation of powers, factions and checks without notes.
OCTOBER

Branches Month

  • Unit 2: Congress
  • Unit 2: Presidency
  • Unit 2: Bureaucracy
  • Unit 2: Judiciary
  • Practice concept application
Checkpoint: Explain how the three branches check each other.
NOVEMBER

Rights Month

  • Complete Unit 3
  • Review civil liberties vs. civil rights
  • Connect required SCOTUS cases to amendments
  • Practice SCOTUS comparison
Checkpoint: Given a scenario, identify the relevant constitutional principle.
DECEMBER

Beliefs Month

  • Complete Unit 4
  • Public opinion + polling
  • Political socialization
  • Liberal vs. conservative policy positions
  • Analyze graphs and polling data
Checkpoint: Read a political graph and explain what it means.
JANUARY

Participation Month

  • Complete Unit 5
  • Voting + elections
  • Political parties
  • Interest groups + media
  • Campaign finance
Checkpoint: Connect political participation to institutions and policy.
FEBRUARY

Connection Month

  • Mix Units 1–5
  • Weekly mixed MCQ set
  • Review every required document
  • Review every required SCOTUS case
  • Build an error log
Checkpoint: Your mistakes should become predictable and fixable.
MARCH

FRQ Month

  • Concept Application FRQ
  • Quantitative Analysis FRQ
  • SCOTUS Comparison FRQ
  • Argument Essay
  • Practice writing under time pressure
Checkpoint: Complete each FRQ type confidently without needing a model answer.
APRIL

Full Exam Month

  • Take timed mixed MCQ sets
  • Complete timed FRQs
  • Review weak units only after testing them
  • Repeat missed questions
  • Do at least 1–2 full exam simulations
Checkpoint: Your practice scores should consistently be in your target range.
MAY

Final Stretch

  • Review your condensed notes
  • Rapid SCOTUS + document review
  • Review your error log
  • Do short MCQ sets, not endless cramming
  • Prioritize sleep and exam readiness
Checkpoint: Walk into the AP exam knowing how to apply what you studied.
